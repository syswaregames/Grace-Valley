Grace Valley has the foundations of a city builder: zoning, roads, power, traffic, a budget, and community needs. I’d focus next on making those systems easier to understand and giving players more interesting ways to respond to problems.

Progress check (November 2026):

1. City overlays - DONE. Map Views includes power, pollution, noise, traffic, and service coverage, with a None option to clear the view.
2. Traffic choices - DONE. Congestion-aware routing lets parallel roads share trips. Connected walking paths replace eligible car commutes, and road-connected shuttle stops carry nearby commuters. Traffic alerts recommend these options.
3. Cleaner power choices - PARTIAL. Solar and wind plants are buildable, with different costs, output, and upkeep and no smoke. The fuel plant remains available. A plant upgrade or replacement progression is still open.
4. Surplus power sales - PLANNED. The Power report shows unused capacity and projected surplus, but there are no neighbor contracts or export income yet.
5. More useful advisor - PARTIAL. A city advisor gives financial-crisis guidance, and the Issues report gives location-aware issues and suggestions. The advisor does not yet explain specific affected neighborhoods or jump the camera to them.
6. Church and community activities - PARTIAL. Gatherings and food drives improve belonging or care and use funds and volunteers. Chapels do not yet offer selectable ongoing programs such as youth activities or outreach.
7. Annual town review - PARTIAL. The calendar and annual report track yearly income, expenses, opening and closing funds, population, and City Score. Building changes, average score, and milestone goals are not yet included.

Next suggested priorities: add neighbor power contracts, then make the annual review and advisor more specific and actionable.
